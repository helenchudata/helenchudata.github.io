Project Summary

Listings123 Host Revenue Analysis

Listings123 needed to identify which hosts to target in an expansion campaign. I cleaned the listing data, summarized results with pivot tables, and compared estimated revenue across cities and room types. San Francisco had the highest total estimated revenue, and entire homes or apartments had the highest average estimated revenue by room type. I recommended focusing the campaign on entire-home hosts and gathering booking and cost data before committing to a market.

Skills and What I Learned

I learned to connect a business question to a measurable outcome and to explain patterns in a way that supports a funding decision. Comparing city totals with per-listing averages also showed me why the level of aggregation matters.

Project Overview

Listings123, a fictional home-sharing company, wanted to expand into new cities. Its CEO needed to show investors what successful hosts had in common before requesting funding for a marketing campaign. I used revenue as the project’s measure of host success and worked through four stages, from defining the business question to presenting recommendations.

Key Findings and Recommendation

$10.59 million
San Francisco’s total revenue, the highest among the cities compared.
$8,644.43
Average revenue for entire homes or apartments, the highest of the room types compared.
R² = 0.509
Average reviews per listing had the strongest relationship with total host revenue among the five variables tested.

My presentation recommended highlighting entire-home listings, 24-hour check-in, and private entrances in the campaign. I also proposed encouraging guests to leave reviews. The patterns are associations from the course data. Booking volume, length of stay, and operating costs would help assess which practices are worth promoting and whether higher revenue translates into higher profit.

Project Reflection

This project helped me connect spreadsheet work to a decision someone actually needed to make. The city comparison also showed me why the level of analysis matters. A city with more listings can lead in total revenue even when its typical listing earns less. I would carry that distinction into an expansion analysis by showing city totals alongside per-listing results.
